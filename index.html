A Whimsical History

Sioux City, Iowa

From prairie rivers to prairie skies — the story of a city, told one flutter at a time.

Sioux City sits at the meeting of the Big Sioux and Missouri Rivers in western Iowa, a location that has shaped its history for millennia. From Indigenous habitation and Lewis and Clark's passage to a 19th-century meatpacking boom, the city's story is one of growth, disaster, and reinvention.

Below: many of the most consequential events in that story, arranged as a timeline.

Pre-history & Early Exploration

  1. c. 9,000 BCE Indigenous habitation begins

    Indigenous peoples inhabit the area where the Big Sioux River meets the Missouri; the site would become home to successive Native American cultures over millennia.

  2. August 20, 1804 Death and burial of Sergeant Charles Floyd

    Sergeant Floyd of the Lewis and Clark Expedition dies, likely of appendicitis, and is buried on a bluff overlooking the Missouri River. He is the only member of the expedition to die on the journey.

  3. 1849 First permanent white settler

    Theophile Bruguier, a French-Canadian fur trader, becomes the first permanent white settler, establishing a farm near the mouth of the Big Sioux River with the family of Yankton Sioux Chief War Eagle.

Founding & Growth (1850s–1870s)

  1. 1854 Sioux City is platted

    Dr. John K. Cook, an English-born physician turned surveyor, officially plats Sioux City, founding the town.

  2. 1857 Incorporation

    Sioux City is incorporated, with a population of about 400.

  3. 1860s Railroads arrive

    The arrival of the railroads begins to transform the town into a regional trade center.

  4. 1868 Stockyards established

    The Sioux City Stockyards are established, laying the foundation for what becomes one of the largest livestock markets in the country.

Meatpacking Boom (1880s–1900s)

  1. 1884 Modern stockyards open

    The modern stockyards open; Sioux City soon becomes the third-largest livestock market in the United States.

  2. 1887–1891 The Corn Palace era

    Sioux City holds its own Corn Palace celebrations, an offshoot of the better-known Mitchell, South Dakota tradition.

  3. 1880s–1890s Packinghouses boom

    Major packinghouses establish operations, including Swift & Company, Armour & Company, Cudahy Packing Company, and Wilson & Company, making Sioux City one of the nation's top five meatpacking cities.

  4. 1892 Great Floyd River Flood

    Heavy rains in May leave at least 3,000 people homeless and cause 25 deaths. Stockyards, rail lines, and lumber yards are badly damaged.

  5. 1893 Financial Panic hits Sioux City

    The nationwide Financial Panic devastates local real-estate investors; developer Edwin Peters reportedly loses $1.5 million.

  6. 1894–1895 Floyd's journals rediscovered

    Floyd's journals are rediscovered in 1894. His grave is rediscovered on Memorial Day 1895, and the Floyd Memorial Association is formed.

  7. 1898 Spanish-American War

    All Iowa National Guard units, including the 51st Infantry, are called up and sent to the Philippines.

  8. 1900 Floyd Monument construction begins

    Construction of the 100-foot Floyd Monument, an Egyptian-style obelisk of Kettle River sandstone, begins on May 29.

  9. 1900 Peirce lottery fraud

    Former land speculator John Peirce runs a nationwide lottery fraud selling tickets for his northside mansion; a New York millionaire pre-arranged as the “winner” is awarded the property.

  10. May 30, 1901 Floyd Monument dedicated

    The Floyd Monument is dedicated, drawing thousands who arrive by special train.

  11. December 23, 1904 Pelletier Department Store fire

    A fire at the Pelletier Department Store guts 2½ blocks of downtown. Millions in damage but only one death; the business district is rebuilt quickly.

  12. 1906 The Jungle and federal reform

    Upton Sinclair publishes The Jungle, exposing meatpacking-industry conditions and leading to the Pure Food and Drug Act and the Federal Meat Inspection Act, which directly affect Sioux City packers.

Early 20th Century (1910s–1930s)

  1. 1910s Modernization

    Sioux City grows rapidly with streetcars, water works, electric lights, and the city's first “skyscrapers.”

  2. 1920s Peak population and economic height

    The city reaches its population and economic peak as a meatpacking and wholesale-trade hub, with a population near 80,000.

  3. 1930s Great Depression

    The Great Depression hits hard; meatpacking employment declines significantly.

Mid-20th Century (1940s–1960s)

  1. 1940s Wartime recovery and the Great Migration

    War-related demand revives the packinghouses. A major wave of African American workers arrives during the Great Migration to fill industrial jobs.

  2. April 1962 Missouri River flood

    A spring flood on the Missouri River inundates downtown Sioux City, causing extensive damage and forcing mass evacuations.

  3. 1960 Floyd Monument becomes a National Historic Landmark

    The Floyd Monument is recognized as a National Historic Landmark by the U.S. Department of the Interior.

Late 20th Century (1970s–1990s)

  1. 1970s–1980s End of the meatpacking era

    The remaining major packinghouses (Swift, Armour) close or consolidate. The city shifts toward food processing and services.

  2. July 19, 1989 United Airlines Flight 232 crash

    A DC-10 suffers a catastrophic engine failure and crash-lands at Sioux Gateway Airport. Of 296 people aboard, 184 survive — one of the most celebrated survivals and crew-resource-management success stories in aviation history (NTSB report DC/AAR-90/06).

21st Century

  1. 2000s–present Economic reinvention

    Sioux City refocuses its economy on healthcare, education, food processing, and tourism; the Orpheum Theatre and other historic structures are restored.

  2. 2010s Downtown revitalization

    Downtown revitalization efforts bring new mixed-use development and riverfront improvements along the Missouri.

  3. 2019 Major Missouri River flooding

    Massive flooding along the Missouri River damages levees and infrastructure; the city coordinates major recovery efforts.

  4. 2020s Continued investment

    Continued investment in the riverfront, biofuels industry, and wind-energy manufacturing in the tri-state region.
